A tooth can sometimes hurt for a few minutes and then feel normal again. But when pain, sensitivity, or swelling keeps returning, it may be a sign that something deeper is happening inside the tooth. A **[root canal](/root-canals-2)** may be recommended when the soft tissue inside a tooth becomes infected or severely damaged.

Not every toothache means you need a root canal. However, knowing the warning signs can help you get the right care at the right time.

* * *

### 1. Tooth Pain That Keeps Coming Back

Pain that continues or returns in the same tooth can be an important warning sign. You may notice an ache while resting, eating, or putting pressure on the tooth.

Even if the pain disappears for a while, the underlying problem may still be present. Repeated tooth pain should be checked by a dentist to find its actual cause.

* * *

### 2. Strong Sensitivity to Hot or Cold

Feeling a brief sensation from a very hot or cold food or drink can happen occasionally. However, if the pain is sharp, intense, or continues after the food or drink is gone, it may point to a problem within the tooth.

Deep decay or damage can affect the pulp, which contains nerves and blood vessels. A dentist can determine whether the sensitivity is caused by a minor issue or something that requires further treatment.

* * *

### 3. Swollen or Tender Gums

An infected tooth can sometimes cause swelling or tenderness around the gums. You may notice that one area feels sore when you touch it or brush your teeth.

Swelling can occur when an infection spreads around the root of a tooth. If it appears along with tooth pain or sensitivity, professional dental care is important.

* * *

### 4. Tooth Discoloration

A tooth that becomes darker or changes color may have experienced damage to the tissue inside it. This can happen after an injury, but it may also be related to decay or infection.

Discoloration does not automatically mean that a root canal is needed. A dental examination can help identify the reason for the color change and determine the appropriate treatment.

* * *

### 5. Pain While Biting or Chewing

Does one tooth hurt whenever you bite down? Pain while chewing can be linked to deep decay, damage, a crack, or inflammation inside the tooth.

You may notice the discomfort when eating harder foods or when pressure is placed on a particular tooth. Ignoring the problem can allow the underlying condition to become more serious.

* * *

### When Should You See a Dentist?

A dentist will examine your tooth and may recommend X-rays or other tests to understand what is causing your symptoms. If the pulp is infected or badly damaged, **[root canal treatment](/root-canals-2)** may help remove the infection while preserving the natural tooth.

Getting dental problems checked early can help prevent unnecessary discomfort and more complicated treatment later. Regular checkups and good oral hygiene also play an important role in protecting your teeth.
